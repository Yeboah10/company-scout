Use cases

Who Company Scout is for, and what it helps them do.

The problem everyone shares

You need to know whether a company is worth your time. Not a Wikipedia summary. Not a chatbot guess. An honest answer, backed by sources you can check, that includes what the public record does not say.

That answer normally takes 2–3 hours of manual work: searching across news, company sites, LinkedIn, funding databases, and investor portals. Company Scout does it in minutes.

Case writers and business school faculty

Find case-worthy companies

The case study score tells you whether a company has the strategic decision, tension, and consequences that make a teaching case work. Companies that do not score well on this are filtered out before you spend a day researching them.

Identify the protagonist

The People tab shows who runs the company, whether their role is current or former, and the best route to reach them. A case needs a decision-maker — not just a company.

Journalists and content strategists

Decide what to write about

The story potential score and the signal ledger tell you whether there is a real story here — a development worth covering, a trajectory worth following, a question worth answering — or just a company doing fine without needing an article about it.

Get the contacts before pitching

Published email addresses, inferred addresses built from real patterns at the company, and LinkedIn profiles. Each contact says what it rests on, so you know what to trust.

Business analysts and consultants

Research a company before a meeting

The intelligence brief gives you the company’s recent developments, strategic signals, and gaps in the public record — the things you need to know that the company’s own website will not tell you.

Compare opportunities

Scout several companies and compare their scores side by side. A company with a high attention score and a low reachability score is a different kind of opportunity from one where both are high.

Investors and venture scouts

Quick-screen a deal

Before spending time on a deep dive, get a brief that tells you what is publicly known, what is missing, and whether there are signals worth following up on. The coverage report shows exactly which research areas came back empty.

Verify claims

Every claim in the brief traces to a dated, quality-rated source. A company that claims a Series A but has no source beyond its own press release looks different from one confirmed by a tier-1 outlet.

Academic researchers

Identify research subjects

The research potential score rates how much information is available, whether there are unresolved questions, and whether the company is complex enough to study. A high score means there is enough material for a paper.

Map the gaps

The coverage report shows which of nine research areas came back empty. Gaps are research opportunities, not failures — a company with no public data on its business model is a question someone should answer.

What it is not

Company Scout is not a CRM, a lead generator, a content engine, or a substitute for talking to people. It is the step before all of those: the thing that tells you whether a company deserves your next three hours. What you do with that answer is your call.
